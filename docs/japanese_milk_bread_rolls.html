Japanese Milk Bread Rolls

Slightly sweet, incredibly soft and airy rolls made with a tangzhong roux that keeps them tender and moist for days.

TIME
3 hrs 57 mins
YIELD
8 to 10 rolls

[+] Ingredients

Tangzhong (starter)

  • 3 tbsp (43g) water
  • 3 tbsp (43g) milk, whole preferred
  • 2 tbsp (14g) bread flour

Dough

  • 2 1/2 cups (300g) bread flour
  • 2 tbsp (14g) dry milk or nonfat dry milk
  • 1/4 cup (50g) granulated sugar
  • 1 tsp (6g) table salt
  • 1 tbsp instant yeast
  • 1/2 cup (113g) cold milk, whole preferred
  • 1 large egg
  • 4 tbsp (57g) unsalted butter, melted

For finishing

  • 1 large egg beaten with 1 tbsp water (egg wash)

[>] Instructions

Weigh your flour; or measure it by gently spooning it into a cup, then sweeping off any excess.

To make the tangzhong: In a small saucepan, combine all of the tangzhong ingredients and whisk until no lumps remain.

Place the saucepan over low heat, and cook the mixture, whisking constantly, until thick and the whisk leaves lines on the bottom of the pan, 3 to 5 minutes.

Transfer the tangzhong to a large mixing bowl, the bowl of a stand mixer, or the bucket of a bread machine (whatever you plan to knead the dough in). Add the cold milk, then the flour and remaining dough ingredients to the mixing bowl in the order listed; the heat from the tangzhong will help to warm the cold milk.

To make the dough: Mix — by hand, on low speed of a stand mixer fitted with the dough hook, or in a bread machine set to the dough cycle — to bring the dough together. Next, knead the dough until it’s smooth, elastic, and tacky. This will take up to 15 minutes by hand, 10 to 12 minutes on medium-low speed of a mixer, or the length of the dough cycle in a bread machine.

Shape the dough into a ball, and let it rest in a lightly greased covered bowl for 1 to 1 1/2 hours, until puffy but not necessarily doubled in bulk.

Gently deflate the dough, divide it into eight equal pieces (about 82g each) for large rolls or 10 equal pieces (about 65g each) for medium-sized rolls, and shape each piece into a ball.

Place the rolls into a lightly greased 8” or 9” round pan. Cover the pan, and let the rolls rest for 40 to 50 minutes, until puffy.

Preheat the oven to 350°F with a rack in the center.

Brush the rolls with the egg wash, and bake for 25 to 30 minutes, until golden brown on top; a digital thermometer inserted into the center of the middle roll should read at least 190°F.

Remove the rolls from the oven. Allow them to cool in the pan for 10 minutes, then transfer to a rack to cool completely before serving.

Store leftover milk bread rolls in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days.
